Ah, if only I had a heart of iron; then steel-willed might I be, steel-tipped
the many daggers of my resolve, steel-plated the plate that bound me. Alas, I
have but bare fabric to shield me amidst this bitter night, and my soul
journeys from fracture to fracture.

What a strange journey the elephant-headed god guides me on—with one hand, he
showers the blessings of opportunity; with the other, he fans the coals
beneath my feet and every step scalds me.

Ah, comfort, what art thou? I had you once, and in embracing you I incurred a
debt; a debt the elephant god so wrathfully exacts from me. Is this usury? Or
must I view this as yet another forge whose heat I must tearfully endure? Is
this the strength I begged him for? One foot in dissolution, another in grief?
Truly, your ways are mysterious, O Gaṇeśa.

We share the same father: Śiva. We share the same shattered tooth—your tusk,
my incisor. We even share our bellies. O friend, O brother in form, what
compels you to burnish such scalding marks upon my skin?

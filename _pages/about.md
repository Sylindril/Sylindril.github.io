I work on agents in the [Machine Learning Department at Carnegie Mellon University](https://www.ml.cmu.edu/), though my interests are still rather versatile. The question I think about most is this: **how do you actually ask good questions?** How do you get a model to come up with questions that are genuinely novel—and scientifically meaningful?

To that end, I'm interested in reinforcement learning, agents, harnesses, and, lately, interpretability—not necessarily in that order. You can find my [CV]({{ '/cv/' | relative_url }}) here, and everything else below.

I find myself spending most of my spare time with a history podcast, a good book, or in the gym. Increasingly, I've been getting back into [writing]({{ '/literary/' | relative_url }}) and theatre. I try to think about more than just AI—about society, history, good cinema, and tasteful prose—not to mention an endless stream of startup ideas of varying quality.

My background in engineering physics has given me a deep appreciation for—and interest in—the quest for an [automated physicist](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-022-00518-3). Wouldn't it be wonderful to see the theory of everything in our lifetimes?
